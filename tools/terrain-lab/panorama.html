Geometry, lighting, and limitations

The 90 m value is used directly as the eye elevation, without adding a person’s height or raising it to nearby ground. Check the reported ground height and datum before interpreting near-field obstructions. Use 92 m to test the alternative “90 m ground + 2 m eye” convention.

Terrain extends 64 km along each local east/north axis, with finer surfaces within 1, 4, and 16 km. Geometry includes Earth curvature with the comparison lab’s fixed terrestrial-refraction coefficient 0.13. Normals follow the rendered grid; fine tiles do not imply that every native terrain detail survives the mesh. Missing triangles remain gaps. No buildings or trees are added.

Sun and Moon directions, phase and distance come from the application’s astronomy routines. Lighting uses slope normals, diffuse daylight, distance haze, two directional shadow maps, and a nonlinear lunar phase law. Only the dominant source casts shadows. Moonlight uses a fixed clear-sky extinction approximation and night exposure; it is a plausible visual model, not a photometric prediction. Off-camera terrain casts shadows, but terrain outside the loaded square cannot. Shadow resolution, bias, mesh transitions, refraction and low-horizon lighting remain approximations. Clouds, snow, reflected light, artificial light, and lunar eclipses are not modelled here.

Mapped water uses the same public-domain Natural Earth ocean and major-lake polygons everywhere. These are generalised at 1:10 million map scale, not detailed shoreline surveys; small lakes may be missing. Water changes colour only: no regional shoreline overrides or assumed lake elevations are applied. The nearest 100 m of terrain is excluded by default (adjustable from 20 to 300 m), with neutral unmodelled foreground. No reflections are modelled.
